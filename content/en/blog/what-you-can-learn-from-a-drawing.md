When you look at your child’s drawing, you might see a house, a sun, and a stick figure. With
a little practice, you can see a lot more — and none of it requires guessing at hidden meanings.

## Seven things a drawing shows

- **Creativity & imagination.** Unusual choices that go past the usual house and sun.
- **Color & light.** How your child picks colors and puts them down.
- **Composition & space.** How much of the page gets used and how the parts fit together.
- **Technique & materials.** How well your child handles a pencil, marker, or brush.
- **Fine motor skills & detail.** Small, precise, repeatable marks.
- **Form & proportions.** How close the shapes and sizes come to the real thing.
- **Story & interaction.** Whether there’s a narrative or characters.

Each of these is **visible**. You’re not guessing at feelings. You’re noticing what your child’s
hand and eye did on the page, against what’s typical for their age.

## From noticing to supporting

The point isn’t to grade your child. It’s to notice a strength and offer a next step. See a
large, confident figure? Try a big sheet and “draw it in one line.” Lots of careful little
patterns? Draw a frame around the page and let them fill it. Only one character and no scene?
Suggest a story where the character meets someone.

A ten-minute invitation like that is usually all it takes.
